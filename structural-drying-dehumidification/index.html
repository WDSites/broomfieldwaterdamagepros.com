The science

How Structural Drying Works

Structural drying in Broomfield, CO is the controlled removal of moisture from framing, subfloor, drywall and concrete using airflow and dehumidification. The plain answer to why it takes equipment is that wet materials release water slowly, and the room air has to stay dry enough to keep accepting it. Fans alone just move damp air around.

Three things work together. Air movers sweep air across wet surfaces, dehumidifiers pull the released vapor out of the air, and meters tell the crew whether it is working. Remove one and the other two slow down.

Our Process: Placement, Readings and Visits

Step 01

Baseline

Meters and thermal imaging map the wet area, and dry reference readings are taken from unaffected materials in the same house.

Step 02

Set up

Air movers and dehumidifiers go in, with doors, closets and wall cavities opened where air must reach.

Step 03

Monitoring visits

A technician returns to re-read the marked points, move equipment as the wet front moves and record the numbers.

Step 04

Reach the goal

Equipment comes out only after readings match the reference materials. Then repair or cleanup can begin.

Drying Goals and Moisture Readings

A drying goal is not a general feeling of dryness. It is a reading, taken from the same kind of material that was never wet, that the affected material has to reach. Wood framing, drywall and concrete each dry at their own pace, so each has its own readings.

If a stud bay or subfloor stays above the goal, the crew changes airflow, opens the cavity or adds dehumidification. For the broader service around this work, see water mitigation.

Safety steps and a checklist around running drying equipment

Drying equipment draws power and runs for days. Never unplug a dehumidifier to charge a phone, never move a cord through standing water, and keep children and pets away from fans and hoses. If water has reached outlets or the panel, call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before calling us.

  • Leave equipment where the crew placed it unless something is unsafe.
  • Keep doors and closets open or closed as instructed.
  • Call if a unit stops, trips a breaker or smells hot.

What Drives the Price of Drying

Wet area

Rooms, wall height and the volume of air to condition set the equipment count.

Material mix

Hardwood, concrete and plaster hold water longer than hollow-core doors or carpet.

Number of visits

Each monitoring visit adds labor, and slower drying means more of them.

Access

Cabinets, tile and finished ceilings make airflow harder and may need holes opened.

How Insurance Treats Drying

Many policies address sudden and accidental water and the drying that follows, while gradual leaks and flood are usually treated differently. Ask your insurer how your loss is classified, and keep the moisture readings and equipment log. Those records show what was wet and when it reached the drying goal.

Mistakes That Stall Drying

  • Opening windows in humid weather, which can bring in more moisture than it removes.
  • Turning off equipment because the room feels dry while framing still reads wet.
  • Covering wet walls with paint, vinyl or cabinetry before readings are done.
  • Leaving standing water behind. Pumping belongs first, see emergency water removal.
