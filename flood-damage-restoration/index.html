When Flood Water Is an Emergency in Broomfield

Flood damage restoration is the removal, drying and cleanup that follows water entering a home from outside or from a failed drain, rather than from a plumbing line you can shut off. In Broomfield, CO that includes stormwater, groundwater seepage and sewer-related backups. Our line is open 24/7, so call as soon as you can do so safely.

While you wait, stay out of standing water if any outlet, appliance or panel is wet, and never touch a wet electrical panel. If the water reaches wiring, call the utility or 911 before you call a restoration company. Lift valuables and keep children and pets away from the water.

Safety first before anything else

Flood water can carry sewage and contaminants, and it can energize wet outlets and appliances. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. If you cannot, leave it and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern.

Broomfield Flood Context

The National Weather Service summary of the September 2013 Front Range flood describes the heaviest northern Denver metro flash flooding on September 15 as falling from Northglenn to around Broomfield. The statewide event covered 18 counties and caused an estimated $2 billion to $3 billion in damage.

That does not mean your street floods. It means a Broomfield homeowner should know the local flood picture. The city's floodplains page covers the City Park Channel and South Tributary floodplains, a floodway between Spader Way and Compton Street, and an online floodplain search tool for any address.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address before the next storm season, and ask your insurer about flood coverage, because standard homeowners policies typically exclude it.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

A flooded basement is two problems at once: the water on the floor and the moisture in the walls and slab. We start by extracting standing water with pumps and truck-mounted or portable extraction units, then remove carpet, pad and any drywall wicked above the waterline.

Moisture meters and thermal imaging map where water traveled, because wall cavities can stay wet long after the floor looks dry. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the space, and crews log readings daily until the structure reaches your home's normal level.

Finished basements add storage, furnishings and electronics to the job. We help you separate what can be cleaned from what must go, and document the loss with photos for your adjuster.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

Water that comes up through a floor drain, tub or toilet is treated as Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which means it is presumed contaminated. Do not wade through it or run a household vacuum on it.

Crews extract the water, remove porous materials such as carpet, pad and low drywall, then clean and disinfect surfaces before drying. If the city sewer main is the source, the City and County of Broomfield lists an after-hours emergency line of 303-438-6400 on its Water Utilities page. Report the backup there while you call us.

Flood coverage and your policy

The Colorado Division of Insurance says flood is typically not a covered event under a homeowners policy, and that living expenses during evacuation are typically not covered either. If you carry separate flood coverage, start that claim as soon as you can. National Flood Insurance Program claims go through floodsmart.gov.

Check your policy for what applies to sewer backup, which is often a separate endorsement, and ask your insurer how your loss will be classified.
