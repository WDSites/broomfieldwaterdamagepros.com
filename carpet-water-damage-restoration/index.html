Carpet basics

Wet Carpet in Broomfield: Dry It or Replace It?

Carpet water damage restoration in Broomfield, CO is the work of deciding whether soaked carpet and pad can be dried where they lie or must come out. The plain answer is that the source of the water and the time it sat matter more than how wet the room looks. Clean supply water caught early often leaves carpet that dries well, while sewage or long-soaked pad usually does not.

Carpet is a layered system. Fibers are held by a backing, the backing rests on a pad, and the pad sits on a subfloor with tack strip around the edges. Water drops into the pad and spreads sideways, so the visible wet patch understates the real one.

First-hour safety steps for soaked carpet

Stop the source before anything else. If water has reached outlets, power strips or lamp cords, stay out of it and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before calling us. A sewage or gas smell means leave the area and keep children and pets away.

  • Shut the fixture valve or the main water shut-off.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms only from a panel you can reach on dry ground.
  • Raise furniture legs onto foil or blocks and clear small rugs and cords.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Call (303) 952-8295 and describe the source of the water.

Our Carpet Drying Process

Step 01

Identify the water

Find the source, judge whether it was clean, gray or contaminated, and meter the carpet, pad and nearby walls.

Step 02

Extract

Extraction units pull water from fibers and pad. For deeper standing water, pumping comes first.

Step 03

Lift and float

Carpet is released from tack strip along one side, the pad comes out if it is saturated, and air movers push air under the carpet.

Step 04

Dry and verify

LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air while meters confirm the subfloor and carpet edges have dried.

Save the Carpet or Replace It

The category of the water decides the first question. Supply water in Broomfield is treated water, but the category depends on where the water has been, not on the tap it came from. Clean water that crosses a dirty floor or sits for days can turn into something carpet cannot be cleaned of.

Pad is the usual casualty because it is a sponge that is hard to rinse. Contaminated water, loosened backing or a subfloor that stayed wet are the reasons to replace the whole piece.

What Changes the Cost of Carpet Work

  • The water category, since contaminated water adds removal and disinfection steps.
  • How much carpet and pad is wet, and whether the damage reaches walls or stairs.
  • Whether the subfloor needs drying or cleaning beneath a removed pad.
  • The number of drying days and equipment pieces the readings call for.

Insurance Fit for Carpet Losses

Sudden and accidental water, such as a supply line failure, is the kind of loss many policies address. Slow leaks, long neglect and flood water are treated differently, and flood is often excluded without separate coverage. Check your policy for how your loss is classified and take photos of the carpet before anything is moved.

Mistakes That Ruin Salvageable Carpet

  • Waiting for the surface to feel dry while the pad stays wet underneath.
  • Walking on soaked carpet, which pushes water deeper and stretches the backing.
  • Heating the room with a space heater and closing it up, which trades drying for odor.
  • Skipping the walls, even though water often wicks up from the carpet edge into drywall.
