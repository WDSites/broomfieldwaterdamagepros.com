Water Is Stopped, Now What? Cleanup in the First 48 Hours

Water damage cleanup is the removal of wet debris and damaged materials, followed by sanitizing and deodorizing the affected area. It happens after the source is stopped, and in Broomfield, CO it is most useful in the first two days, before odors and bacteria set in. Our line is open 24/7 for emergency cleanup.

While you wait for a crew, shut off the water source, lift furniture and valuables off wet floors, open interior doors for air movement and avoid running a household vacuum on wet carpet. If the water may be contaminated, keep children and pets away.

What Gets Removed and What Gets Saved

Cleanup decisions follow the water category and how long materials stayed wet. The goal is to keep what can be dried and cleaned, and remove what cannot be made safe.

Contaminated water includes sewage and flood water. Categories follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

How Cleanup Is Done

Step 01

Walk-through

Identify the water category, set the boundary of the affected area and photograph conditions for your records.

Step 02

Remove

Take out wet debris, damaged flooring, pad and drywall that cannot be saved, and bag it for disposal.

Step 03

Clean

Wash surfaces, wipe hard materials and apply an antimicrobial where the water category calls for it.

Step 04

Deodorize

Treat lingering odor with air filtration and deodorizing methods, then hand off to drying and repair.

Contents, Odor and the Other Half of Cleanup

Contents are the hardest part of the first week. Clothing, books, cardboard boxes and stored paper wick water and hold smell. Hard goods usually clean up well, while soft goods that sat in contaminated water are often discarded.

Odor is a signal. A musty or sour smell after cleanup means moisture is still in a material, so we check with meters before deciding whether more drying or mold work is needed. A smell that persists after drying may also point to mold remediation.

Insurance note

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded without a separate flood policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer how your loss is classified.
