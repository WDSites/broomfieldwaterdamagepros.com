When Standing Water Is an Emergency

Emergency water removal means pumping and extracting standing water from a home as fast as safely possible. In Broomfield, CO that could be a burst pipe under a sink, a failed water heater, an overflowing tub or a flooded crawl space. Our 24/7 line is open now.

Before you call, shut off the main water valve or the fixture valve. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Lift valuables and keep people and pets out of the water. For any electrical or gas concern, call the utility or 911 first.

How Water Extraction Works

Pumps handle deep water, and truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull water out of carpet, pad and hard surfaces. Removing water mechanically is far faster than drying it, because every gallon taken out is a gallon the dehumidifiers never have to handle.

Once the standing water is gone, the crew uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that moved into walls, under cabinets and below flooring. That map decides where structural drying has to continue.

Burst Pipe Water Damage

The City and County of Broomfield notes on its water utilities page that cold winters mean frozen pipes that burst and cause excessive damage. Denver-area weather records put the average first freeze around October 7 and the last around May 5, so the risk window is long.

A pipe that burst inside an exterior wall, an attic or a garage can run for hours before anyone notices. Find the main water shut-off and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is. Shut it first, then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain remaining water.

After the water stops, extraction begins and the pipe repair is handled by a licensed plumber. We document the damage for your insurer and keep the removed materials until your adjuster has seen them. A burst pipe is usually a sudden and accidental loss, which many homeowners policies cover, so check your policy.

Stay out if electricity is involved

Water and electricity do not mix. If water is touching outlets, appliances or the panel, do not stand in it. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line.

Crawl Spaces and Whole-Room Flooding

Water in a crawl space is easy to miss. A broken irrigation line, a leaking supply pipe or surface water can pool under a house for days. Left in place, it saturates insulation and wood framing and feeds odor and mold.

Crews pump the crawl space, remove wet insulation and set drying equipment that works in a tight space. If the whole level of a house is flooded, extraction runs room by room, and we separate what can be dried from what must be removed. For storm-driven flooding and basements, see flood damage restoration.

The Standard Behind the Work

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it sorts water into categories that decide what can be dried and what must be removed. Ask any company whether its crews work to it, and ask how it will tell you which category applies to your loss.
