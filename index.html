First hour

Emergency Water Damage in Broomfield: What to Do in the First Hour

Emergency water damage is any loss where water is still spreading or where wet materials will be ruined within days. The first steps are simple and they decide how much has to be removed later.

Do these while you call the 24/7 line, and call us as soon as it is safe to do so.

01

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main water shut-off. Everyone in the house should know where it is.

02

Cut power safely

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground.

03

Lift and protect

Raise valuables, rugs and furniture off wet floors, and keep children and pets away.

04

Photograph the damage

Take photos and video before anything is moved, for your insurer.

05

Call the 24/7 line

Describe what you see and we will explain safe next steps.

Electrical or gas risk? Call the utility or 911 first

If water touches outlets, appliances or the panel, do not stand in it. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern before you call a restoration company.

Local risk factors

Why Water Damage Happens in Broomfield

The City and County of Broomfield notes on its water utilities page that cold winters mean frozen pipes that burst and cause excessive damage. Denver-area weather records put the average first freeze around October 7 and the last around May 5, so a long stretch of the year can freeze exposed lines. Know your main shut-off before the first cold night.

Floods are part of the picture too. The National Weather Service describes the heaviest northern Denver metro flash flooding of September 2013 as falling from Northglenn to around Broomfield, and Broomfield's floodplain page covers the City Park Channel and South Tributary floodplains. Check your address with the city's floodplain search tool and the FEMA flood map, and ask your insurer about flood coverage.

Housing age matters. City history pages date Broomfield Heights to 1955, Interlocken's first portions to 1983 and 1986, and Anthem and Arista to 2001 through 2012. Older plumbing tends to fail at joints and valves, while newer homes often leak at appliance lines and condensate drains.

Commercial and multi-tenant buildings, including offices around Interlocken and retail near FlatIron Crossing, add another pattern. Water can travel between suites through shared ceilings and walls, so a loss in one unit can become several.
