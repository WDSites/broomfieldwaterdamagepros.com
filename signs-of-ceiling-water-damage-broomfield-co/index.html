Signs of Ceiling Water Damage in Broomfield, CO and What to Do First

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

The signs of ceiling water damage in Broomfield, CO are brown or yellow stains, sagging drywall, bubbling paint, drips and a musty smell. Each one tells you that water has reached the ceiling layer and may still be arriving. A ceiling that is wet, bulging or near a light fixture is urgent. A dry, faded stain can wait a few days for a proper look.

Short answer

If the ceiling is sagging, dripping or has a fixture in the wet area, keep people out from under it and deal with electrical risk first. If it is only stained and feels dry, find the source before anyone paints.

What to look for

Six Signs That Water Is in Your Ceiling

Ceilings hide water well. Drywall soaks it up, insulation holds it, and the stain on the surface is often much smaller than the wet area above it.

Stains with a ring

Yellow or brown patches, often with a darker edge, mean water has travelled through the board and dried at the rim. A new ring around an old stain means the leak came back.

Sagging or bulging

Wet drywall loses strength and bellies downward. A bulge can hold a surprising amount of water, so treat it as a hazard until it is released safely.

Bubbling or peeling paint

Paint lifts when moisture pushes from behind. Soft blisters that feel damp are a sign of an active source, not an old one.

Drips and beading

Drops at a seam, a vent or around a recessed light show where water has found a path out. Place a bucket, then look upstairs or in the attic.

Musty smell

A damp, earthy odor in a room with no visible stain can mean wet insulation or drywall above. Smell is a clue to measure, not a verdict.

Spots near fixtures

Dark marks around a light, fan or smoke detector deserve extra care because water and wiring share the same cavity.

Your First 24 Hours, in Order

The goal on day one is to stop more water from arriving and to limit what the wet area can ruin. You do not need tools beyond a bucket, a camera and the shutoff valve you already own.

Step 01

Make the room safe

Move people and pets out from under the damaged area. Stay clear of any ceiling that is visibly bowed.

Step 02

Stop the supply

If a pipe, toilet or tub is the likely source, close its shutoff or the main valve. If rain or snowmelt is the source, you cannot stop it, so move to containment.

Step 03

Catch and protect

Set buckets under drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics aside. Cover what cannot move with plastic.

Step 04

Photograph everything

Take wide and close photos of the stain, the room and any damaged belongings before anything is touched. Note the date and when you first saw it.

Step 05

Call for a moisture check

A restoration crew can map the wet area with a moisture meter and thermal imaging and start drying. Call (303) 952-8295 and describe what you see.

Electrical or gas risk comes first

If water is dripping from a light fixture, fan or outlet, or you smell gas, leave the area and do not touch switches. Turn off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry and safely. Electrical or gas danger goes to 911 or your utility, and the restoration line comes after that.

Is the Water Clean? Why It Matters for the Ceiling

Broomfield's drinking water comes from Denver Water plus Colorado-Big Thompson and Windy Gap supplies treated at the city's own plant. The city's water quality report and FAQ say home treatment is not needed for health reasons. That speaks to the water at your tap, not to what a leak has touched on its way down through the ceiling.

A fresh supply line leak starts as clean water, but it picks up dust, insulation fibers and anything in the cavity. Water that came from a roof, a drain line or a toilet is a different matter. Tell the crew where you think it came from, since that decides what can be dried in place and what must be removed under the S500 water categories.

Active Leak or Old Stain? Quick Checks

A stain with a crisp, dark ring that feels cool and slightly soft is usually active. A pale stain that feels dry and has not grown over several weeks is more likely old. A moisture meter gives a better answer than a fingertip, which is why a reading is worth having before you repaint.

  • Press gently near the edge. Soft or spongy board means moisture is still inside.
  • Run the upstairs shower or flush the toilet above and watch the stain for several minutes.
  • Check the attic or the roofline after rain or a thaw.
  • Trace a pencil line around the stain and check it again the next day.
Local note

Broomfield note: cold months and burst pipes

The City and County of Broomfield Water Utilities page says cold winters bring frozen pipes that burst and cause excessive damage. NWS Denver/Boulder records show freezing weather arriving in early autumn and lingering into spring, so the risk window is long. If you leave a house cool or travel in that stretch, ask someone to check ceilings and walls while you are away.

If water is coming from the street side of your property or you cannot tell whose pipe is leaking, the city lists an after-hours line at 303-438-6400 for urgent problems. Non-urgent concerns go through the online Submit a Water/Sewer Concern form. Close your own main first, then call the right party with a clear description.

What Happens After You Call

We measure first. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show how far the water spread beyond the stain. If drywall has softened or insulation is saturated, it is removed and the cavity is dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers before anything is rebuilt.

We handle the drying and the rebuild, which includes new board, texture and paint through our ceiling repair work. Pipes and shingles are other trades, so if the source is a failed pipe or a shingle line, a plumber or roofer fixes that part. If the leak is still running when we arrive, our emergency water removal service comes first.

Wet drywall and insulation that stay damp can grow mold, so speed matters. If growth is already visible, see our mold remediation page for how that work is scoped. We also respond to calls in Westminster and Louisville, and in Lafayette and Erie.

Insurance and Records

Insurers often cover a burst pipe and often do not cover a slow leak left alone. Read your policy and ask what it says about the cause of yours. Your photos, dates and the moisture readings help describe what happened, and nobody can promise how a claim will be decided.
