Wet Carpet, Soft Walls or a Musty Smell? That Is a Mitigation Call

If the carpet squishes, a wall feels soft, paint is bubbling or a room smells musty after a leak, water is still inside your materials. The trade name for stopping that damage is water mitigation, and the first days decide how much needs to be rebuilt.

Our 24/7 line is open now, and a quick call can save materials that would be lost by waiting a week.

What Emergency Water Mitigation Means in Broomfield, CO

Water mitigation is the work done in roughly the first 24 to 72 hours to stop water damage from spreading. It starts with finding and stopping the source, then removing standing water, mapping moisture and starting structural drying.

It is different from repair, which rebuilds after the structure is dry, and different from cleanup, which removes and sanitizes affected materials. Mitigation is the part that protects what can still be saved.

01

Stop the source

Close the fixture shut-off or the main water valve if the supply line is leaking.

02

Cut power safely

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. Otherwise call the utility.

03

Lift and protect

Raise furniture, rugs and valuables onto blocks or off the floor and keep people and pets out.

04

Photograph it

Take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved, for your insurer.

How Mitigation Works, Step by Step

Step 01

Source and safety

Identify the water source and category, check for electrical risk and set up protection.

Step 02

Extraction

Remove standing water with pumps and extraction units, since evaporation alone is slow and puts moisture into the air.

Step 03

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and thermal imaging trace water into walls, subfloors and ceilings so drying is aimed at the real wet areas.

Step 04

Drying setup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in, and readings are logged daily until the structure reaches its dry standard.

Structural Drying

Structural drying is the equipment phase of mitigation. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, and low grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into the materials. Moisture meters guide how many machines run and for how long.

Drying time depends on the materials and how much water they absorbed, so it can be a few days or longer. Hardwood, plaster and thick insulation hold water longer than carpet. The crew checks readings on every visit and removes equipment only when the numbers say the structure is dry.

Cold Colorado air matters here, because Denver-area records show the first freeze arriving around early October and the last in early May. A house with the heat turned down during a freeze can have a leak and a slow dry at the same time, so crews measure rather than guess.

Insurance note

Mitigation records are the documentation your insurer will look at first. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood coverage. Ask your insurer how your loss is classified and keep every log and photo.

Early mitigation also helps limit the damage that is claimed, which matters under most policies.

The Standard Behind the Work

Professional drying is described in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its crews work to it, and ask how drying will be confirmed. A dry reading, not a calendar day, decides when equipment comes out.
