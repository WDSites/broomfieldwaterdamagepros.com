Sewage Backup in Broomfield, CO: What It Is and What to Do First

A sewage backup in Broomfield, CO is wastewater that comes up through a floor drain, tub, toilet or sink instead of going down. It is treated as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category, which means it is handled with containment and protective gear. The plain answer is that you should stay out of it and call.

Backups often show first in the lowest drain in the house, so a basement floor drain or a bathtub in a lower level is the usual place to look.

Safety steps before anyone touches sewage water

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, call the utility or 911 for the electrical risk before calling us. A gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. Do not wade into sewage water, and do not run washers, dishwashers or toilets while the line is blocked.

  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the area.
  • Stop using water fixtures so you do not add more wastewater.
  • Wear waterproof gloves and boots if you must cross the area, and wash well afterward.
  • Photograph what you see before anything is moved.
  • Call the city for a main line problem, and call us for the cleanup.

Who Is Responsible for the Line

The City and County of Broomfield describes the pipes that connect a building's plumbing to the city sewer as Property Service Connections. The city page does not say who pays for a backup, so ask the city and your insurer directly.

For an urgent problem with the public system, the city's Water Utilities page lists an after-hours emergency line at 303-438-6400. Non-urgent concerns go through its online Submit a Water/Sewer Concern form. A plumber can find out whether the blockage is in your line or the main.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference that defines water categories 1 to 3. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Insurance Fit: Backup, Flood and Plumbing

Sewer or drain backup is often treated separately from other water losses, and some policies add it as an endorsement. Ask your insurer whether your policy covers it.

The Colorado Division of Insurance says an insurer must provide a copy of your policy within 3 business days of a request. Check your policy for its notice requirements, keep photos and call your insurer early.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Backup

  1. Cleaning it yourself with a household mop and bucket. That spreads contamination.
  2. Running a household fan, which can move airborne particles through the house.
  3. Keeping soaked carpet or pad because it looks fine. Porous materials hold contamination.
  4. Pouring drain cleaner into a backed-up line.
  5. Waiting to call. The longer wet materials sit, the more of them have to go.
