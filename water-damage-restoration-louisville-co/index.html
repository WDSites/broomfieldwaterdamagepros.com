Coal Creek and flooding

What Louisville Says About Its Own Flood Risk

The City of Louisville's floodplain page calls Coal Creek the best-known flood source, and adds that flooding also occurs near drainage facilities, irrigation ditches and some city streets. It says the September 2013 floods showed that parts of the city are prone to flooding.

To find out whether your property is in a floodplain, the city says to contact its Planning Division at 303-335-4592. Floodplain maps and elevation certificates are available there, and FEMA's flood map site is another route. The city also states that all types of construction on a property in a floodplain need a floodplain development permit, including minor work like a deck.

The page adds that if improvements to a home or commercial building exceed 50 percent of the building's value, the building must be elevated above the base flood elevation. That matters after a big loss, because a major rebuild can trigger the rule. Ask the city before you sign a repair contract.

Louisville participates in the National Flood Insurance Program, and the city says standard homeowner's insurance does not cover flood damage. Renters can buy flood coverage for their belongings. Our flood damage restoration page explains what we do once creek or storm water is in the house.

Permits

Building Safety and Rebuild Steps in Louisville

The city's Building Safety Division at 749 Main St oversees permits and inspections and can be reached at (303) 335-4584 or building@louisvilleco.gov. Permits are handled through the city's Customer Self Service portal. Drying comes first and permits belong to the rebuild stage, so see our water damage repair page for how the two fit together.
