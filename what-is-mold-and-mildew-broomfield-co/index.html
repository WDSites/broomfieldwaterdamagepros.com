What Is Mold and Mildew? A Plain Guide for Broomfield, CO Homes

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

Mold is a type of fungus that grows on damp surfaces, and mildew is the term most people use for light mold growth on surfaces like bathroom tile and window sills. In Broomfield, CO the practical difference matters less than the cause, because both need moisture to grow. Find the water, dry the surface and clean or remove what grew.

Definitions

Mold Versus Mildew in General Terms

Archived EPA guidance treats mildew as a type of mold or surface growth, and notes it is often the word used for light growth in bathrooms. That is why you may hear both words for the same gray, white or dark film. Do not rely on color to name it.

The CDC says mold is present indoors and outdoors and grows where there is moisture. So the question is rarely whether spores exist in your house. The better question is whether any surface stays wet long enough for them to settle in.

How the two words are usually used
TraitMildew (usual use)Mold (usual use)
Where it showsSurface film on tile, grout, sills and shower curtainsSurface or inside materials such as drywall, ceiling tile and wood
How it looksFlat, powdery or patchy, often light in colorFuzzy, spotted or stained, in many colors
Typical handlingWipe with detergent and water, then dryFix the water, then clean or remove depending on the material

These are everyday labels, not lab categories. A professional treats growth by where it is and how wet the material got.

Where Mold Grows Inside a House

Growth follows moisture, and moisture follows a few predictable paths. Check these places first before you assume the problem is bigger than it is.

Bathrooms

Showers, fan ducts and ceilings stay damp after use. Grout lines and caulk joints catch the film first.

Under sinks

A weeping trap or supply fitting can wet the cabinet floor for weeks. Look with a flashlight and feel for swollen particleboard.

Basements and lower levels

Cool concrete and closed storage invite condensation. Boxes pushed against an exterior wall can stay damp unseen.

Windows

Condensation on glass drips onto sills and frames, and cold-weather moisture can leave dark edges.

Behind appliances

Washers, dishwashers and refrigerator water lines leak slowly behind a finished panel.

Attics and HVAC

Roof leaks, poor ventilation and condensate pans can wet insulation and ducts above the living space.

Four Moisture Sources That Are Easy to Miss

01

Slow plumbing leaks

A drip at a fitting or a failing seal can run for weeks. Check shutoff valves, toilet bases and tub drains for dampness or staining.

02

Humidity from daily life

Showers, cooking and drying clothes indoors add moisture. Run fans and keep air moving.

03

Roof and window entry

Snowmelt and rain find gaps at flashing and frames. Look for ceiling stains after storms.

04

Past water events

Wet drywall or carpet that was not dried fully can stay damp. If a leak was never dried properly, treat the area as suspect.

How a Single Wet Event Turns Into Growth

A spill or a leak does not become a mold problem on its own schedule. It becomes one when wet material stays wet. Drywall paper, wood, carpet backing and insulation all hold water, and growth needs both that water and something to feed on.

That is why drying speed matters more than which product you wipe on. Pull wet rugs and boxes off the floor, run fans, open the area to air and get a reading on anything that still feels cool or damp. If the water was large or reached insulation or the wall cavity, emergency water removal and structural drying are the safer route.

What a surface clean does not fix

A wipe-down changes how a wall looks. It does not tell you whether the wall behind it is still wet. Verify with a moisture meter before you decide the job is done.

What Is Safe to Handle Yourself

For a small patch, the EPA's line sits near 10 square feet, around 3 ft on each side, and below it most people can do the cleanup themselves. Hard surfaces get detergent and water and a thorough drying. Soft items such as ceiling tiles and carpet might need to be thrown out if they grow mold.

Fix leaks first. A clean surface that keeps getting wet can grow again. No cleaner holds up if the moisture source is still there. Paint and caulk belong on clean, dry surfaces, so finish the cleanup first.

When It Needs a Professional

  • The growth covers more than about 10 square feet or keeps spreading.
  • Drywall, insulation or carpet was soaked by a leak, flood or sewage.
  • You see growth around vents, ducts or inside the air handler.
  • A musty smell stays strong after the area looks dry.
  • Someone in the home has a health concern. Ask a physician about that, and have the moisture checked.

A professional visit starts with moisture meters and thermal imaging to find where the water went, then containment barriers, negative air machines and HEPA air filtration when removal is needed. Our mold remediation service follows that order, and water mitigation comes first if the area is still wet. A lab test is no substitute for drying the structure, and basement waterproofing is outside our work.

Local note

Broomfield note: you probably own the problem

Census QuickFacts reports 62.7 percent of Broomfield households are owner-occupied, out of 31,760 households. If you own your home, nobody else will notice a damp closet or a weeping cabinet for you. Build a habit of checking under sinks, behind toilets and around windows every season.

The same figures put median owner-occupied home value at $664,500 for 2020 to 2024, which makes early action on a small leak worth more than a late fix on a large one. If you rent, report moisture to your landlord in writing and keep a copy.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire Anyone

Ask for a written scope that names the area, the method and how drying will be confirmed. Ask which published standard the crew follows for mold remediation, and ask whether your state licenses mold work. A company that explains scope and price before work starts is easier to compare than one that quotes a total over the phone.

Also ask what the company does not do. Plumbing and basement waterproofing are not services we offer, so a plumber or a drainage specialist may still be part of the fix. Keep every document, since your insurer may ask what happened and what was done.

A Short Prevention Routine

Wipe condensation from windows and tile, run the bathroom fan after showers and keep furniture a few inches from exterior walls. Dry spills and wet items within a day. If you see staining, measure before you assume, and call us if the reading is high.
