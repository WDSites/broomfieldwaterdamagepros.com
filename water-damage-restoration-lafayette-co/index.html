Lafayette Water Damage: Who Does What

Lafayette, Colorado has its own city government at 1290 S. Public Road, its own water, wastewater and stormwater billing, and a population the Census Bureau estimates at about 30,100. Broomfield Water Damage Pros responds to Lafayette calls in ZIP code 80026 from our office at 2095 W 6th Ave.

When water gets into a Lafayette home, two different groups have jobs. The city looks after its pipes, mains and sewers, and a restoration company deals with damage inside the building. Knowing which is which saves time, so this page starts with the city's contact points.

Coal Creek and Rock Creek

Creek Floods and Low Areas

An older federal Flood Insurance Study for Lafayette states that flooding caused by overflow of Coal Creek and Rock Creek was studied in detail. It says the two watersheds drain about 79 square miles, mostly in southeast Boulder County, and that Rock Creek joins Coal Creek just east of Lafayette. The study dates to 1979, so treat it as background and check current maps for your address.

The city also runs monthly sanitary sewer jetting of known low-lying areas, which tells you the sewer system has spots that need regular attention. Its Water Reclamation Facility has operated since 1962 and releases treated water to Coal Creek. If you live in a low area, ask the city whether it is on that list.

Old town and mine ground

A Coal Town Underneath

Lafayette's residents voted 31 to 28 on April 2, 1889 to incorporate, and coal mining shaped the town for decades. The city's heritage pages say most of old town was undercut by mines, and that development was restricted for a time in some areas. A Lafayette West Subdivision was platted in 1893, west of Public Road between Geneseo and Cleveland streets.

For water damage, age matters more than history. Older homes tend to have original plumbing and drains, and newer areas tend to have appliance connections and condensate lines as the common failure points. Census data lists the median value of an owner-occupied home at about $686,500 and the owner-occupied rate at 65.7 percent, so both owners and renters call us.

Bills and permits

Utility Bills, Leaks and Permits

The city's utility billing page describes a leak adjustment that covers one month, available once every three years. If a hidden leak ran up your bill, ask the city whether you qualify. The Building Division at 303-661-1451 or buildingpermits@lafayetteco.gov can tell you which repairs need permits before you rebuild.

Drying and cleanup come first, and rebuild comes after. Our water mitigation, water damage cleanup and water damage repair pages walk through each step.
