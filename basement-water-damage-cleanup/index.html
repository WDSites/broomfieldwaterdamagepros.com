Why Basements Take On Water in Broomfield, CO

Basement water damage in Broomfield, CO is wet flooring, wall framing and drywall below grade, and the fix is pumping, removing what stays wet and drying the rest. The water may come from a supply line, a drain, a failed sump pump or rain and snowmelt moving through the ground.

Water settles at the lowest point of a house, so a small leak upstairs can end up soaking the lower level. Finished basements hide the problem behind carpet, pad and paneled walls, which is why a damp smell is often the first clue.

First-hour safety steps for a flooded basement

Do not walk into standing water if the panel, outlets or a furnace are in the basement. Water touching electrical equipment is a job for the utility or 911 before you call us. A gas smell means leave the house and call the utility or 911.

  • Stop the source if it is a supply line by closing the main or fixture valve.
  • Stay off the stairs if the bottom steps are underwater.
  • Move boxes, electronics and furniture to higher ground only from dry footing.
  • If sewage may be involved, keep children and pets upstairs.

How We Clean Out and Dry a Basement

Step 01

Pump and extract

Submersible pumps clear standing water and extraction units pull it from carpet and pad. Deep or storm-driven water belongs with emergency water removal.

Step 02

Strip out

Wet carpet, pad and drywall that stayed wet are cut out and bagged. Wall cavities are opened so they can breathe.

Step 03

Map moisture

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water traveled behind finished walls and under flooring.

Step 04

Dry in place

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings settle, with HEPA air filtration if the demolition raised dust.

Step 05

Hand off

We document what was removed. A plumber, waterproofing contractor and rebuilder take it from there.

Basement Mistakes to Avoid

  • Running a household vacuum on wet carpet, which is a shock risk and does not remove water from the pad.
  • Leaving carpet in place for days because the surface feels dry.
  • Closing finished walls over wet insulation or drywall.

What Changes the Cost of a Basement Job

No two basements price alike. The depth and volume of water, how much of the level is finished, the number of wet wall sections and whether sewage was involved all change the scope. Large unfinished spaces usually need less tear-out than a finished basement with built-in cabinetry. We explain the scope before work starts.

Insurance for Basement Water: Sudden, Gradual or Flood

The source decides the policy question. The Colorado Division of Insurance says flood is typically not a covered event under a homeowners policy, while a sudden pipe failure may be treated differently. An insurer must give you a copy of your policy within 3 business days of a request, so ask for it and check its notice requirements.
