What a burst pipe is

Burst Pipe Water Damage in Broomfield, CO

Burst pipe water damage in Broomfield, CO is the harm done when a pressurized supply line or fitting inside a home splits and releases water into walls, floors or ceilings. The plain answer is that the water keeps flowing until someone closes a valve, so speed matters more than anything else.

Most failures start at a joint, a worn connector or a section that froze earlier. Because the line is under pressure, a small split can wet an entire room before anyone hears it. If the cause was a freeze, our page on frozen pipe water damage covers the thaw stage.

First-hour safety steps when a pipe lets go

Your pipe is on your side of the meter, so the city is not the first call. Work through these steps in order.

  • Close the main water valve, usually near where the line enters the house, or the nearest fixture valve.
  • If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out of it and call the electric utility or 911 before calling us.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest level to drain what is left in the lines.
  • Lift valuables, then call a plumber for the repair and call us for the water.

Reading the Signs a Pipe Has Failed

Signs of a failed pipe
What you noticeWhat it usually means
Sound of running water with every tap closedA line is leaking under pressure somewhere
Ceiling stain that spreads or sagsA line in the floor above or in the attic is wet
Warm or damp spot on the floorA supply line may be running through a slab or subfloor
Water meter keeps movingThe leak is active and the main should be closed
Musty smell near a wallMoisture has been inside the cavity for a while

These are clues only. A moisture meter and thermal imaging confirm where the water went.

Insurance and a Sudden Pipe Break

Check the wording first

A sudden break is often treated differently from a slow drip that went unnoticed, but policies vary. Check your policy for how it words sudden and accidental loss, and ask your insurer what it wants documented.

Photograph the damage before anything is removed. Plumbing damage and flood damage are usually separate questions, since flood is typically not covered by a homeowners policy.

Mistakes That Make a Pipe Break Worse

  1. Waiting to see whether the leak slows down before closing the valve.
  2. Running a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  3. Hiding a wet wall behind new paint, which traps moisture.
  4. Skipping the repair permit question. Ask the city before a rebuild.
