Water Damage Insurance Claims in Broomfield, CO: What to Know First

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

A water damage insurance claim in Broomfield, CO is a request to your insurer to pay for a covered loss, and your policy decides what counts as covered. The cause of the water matters more than the amount of it. We are a restoration company, not an insurer, so this page covers general terms and tells you what to ask.

Short answer

Read your policy, stop further damage, document before cleanup and keep every record.

  • Sudden and accidental water events are often covered.
  • Gradual leaks and neglect are often not.
  • Flood usually needs separate flood coverage.
  • Check your policy and ask your insurer, because wording varies.
Step one

Find Out What Kind of Water Event It Was

Insurers sort water losses by cause. A burst pipe, a failed supply hose or an appliance that suddenly overflows is usually described as sudden and accidental. A slow drip behind a wall that ran for months is a different conversation.

Write down what happened, when you noticed it and what you did next. Those facts shape how the loss is described. Do not guess at a cause in writing if you do not know it. Say what you saw and let the plumber or adjuster identify the source.

What Is Often Covered and What Often Is Not

Policies differ, so treat this table as a way to prepare questions, not as a coverage decision. The words in your own policy control.

Request Your Policy in Writing

The Colorado Division of Insurance says an insurer must provide a copy of your policy within 3 business days of a request. Ask for the full policy with endorsements, not just the declarations page. Read the sections on water, mold, flood, sewer backup and your duties after a loss.

The same division notes that flood is typically not a covered event under a homeowners policy, and that living expenses during evacuation are typically not covered. If you have questions about your rights, its Consumer Services line is 303-894-7490 or 800-930-3745.

Documentation That Helps Everyone

01

Photos and video before cleanup

Walk every affected room and show the source, the waterline and each wet material.

02

A written timeline

Note when the water was discovered, when you shut it off and who you called.

03

Moisture readings and notes

Ask for dated readings from moisture meters. They show how the structure dried.

04

Receipts and removed items

Keep emergency purchase receipts and hold samples of removed materials if the insurer asks.

Do not throw away damaged materials before your insurer has had a chance to ask for photos or samples. If something has to go quickly for safety, such as soaked pad or contaminated material, photograph it first.

Safety comes before paperwork

Hazards come first. When water has reached outlets, a panel or appliances, or sewage is involved, phone the utility or 911 about the electrical or gas danger, then reach a restoration company. The paperwork can wait until the hazard is handled.

Reduce Further Damage Without Making Things Worse

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Stopping the water, moving belongings and starting drying are normal examples. Our emergency water removal work is aimed at exactly that stage.

Professional drying is organized around ANSI/IICRC S500, a published standard that sorts water into categories 1 to 3 and damage into classes 1 to 4. A company can tell you whether its crews follow it. Our water damage cleanup scope uses moisture meters, thermal imaging, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and we write down what we find.

Local note

Broomfield note: start early, check the details

If you have flood coverage, the Colorado Division of Insurance says to start the claim as soon as you can. NFIP claims go through floodsmart.gov. No claim deadline is stated on the division's page, so read your policy for its notice requirements and call your insurer promptly.

Rebuild work has a local layer too. The city's Building Division handles permits for drywall and flood repairs, so ask it what applies before a contractor opens a wall. Your insurer and your contractor will both need a clear scope.

Two Phases, Two Sets of Records

A water loss usually has an emergency phase and a rebuild phase. The first covers extraction, tear-out and drying, and the second covers drywall, flooring and paint. Ask your insurer whether it wants separate estimates for each, and keep the paperwork for the two phases apart.

Living expenses are a common surprise. If you have to leave the house for a while, ask your insurer before you book anywhere, since the division says living expenses during evacuation are typically not covered. Knowing that early lets you plan instead of reacting.

Mistakes That Complicate a Claim

  • Waiting days to report a loss because the damage looked small.
  • Tearing out and discarding materials with no photos.
  • Signing a contract or work authorization you have not read.
  • Assuming a verbal answer from anyone is the same as written coverage.
  • Letting a wet area sit while you debate coverage. Drying delays can make damage worse, and that can raise questions.

Most of these come down to timing and records. A claim is easier to explain when you can show what you saw, when you saw it and what you did next. Even a denied or partial claim goes better when your notes are clear.

Keep one running log of calls with your insurer: the date, the person you spoke with and what was said. Follow up important calls with a short email. That log is often the document that settles a later disagreement.
