How to Clean Mold in Your Broomfield, CO Home: What the EPA Says

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

To clean mold in a Broomfield, CO home, fix the water source first, then scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water and dry them completely. The EPA says that if the moldy area is less than about 10 square feet, roughly a 3 ft by 3 ft patch, you can in most cases handle the job yourself. Larger growth, hidden growth and sewage are jobs for a professional.

Short answer

Small, visible patch on a hard surface: you can usually clean it. Larger than about 10 square feet, soaked materials, HVAC involvement or sewage: stop and call.

Step zero

Stop the Water Before You Pick Up a Brush

The EPA's advice opens with the cause: fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible, and dry all items completely. Cleaning a surface that is still being rewetted is wasted work. Find the leak, close the valve and let air move through the area.

If you cannot find the source, a moisture meter and thermal imaging can trace it. That is a separate step from cleaning, and it is often the one that decides whether the growth returns. We offer that tracing as part of our water mitigation work.

Protect Yourself First

Gloves

Use rubber or nitrile gloves that cover the wrist. Wash hands after you finish.

Eye protection

Goggles that seal around the eyes keep spores and splashes out, especially when working overhead.

Respiratory protection

Many people wear a fitted respirator when disturbing growth. Check the product label and ask a physician if you have a lung condition or allergy.

Ventilation and isolation

Open a window, use a fan that exhausts outside and close the door to the room. Keep the HVAC system off while you work.

The Cleaning Sequence for a Small Patch

01

Confirm the size

Measure the area. If it is less than about 10 square feet and the surface is firm, you are in the EPA's range for self-cleanup.

02

Scrub hard surfaces

Use detergent and water on tile, sealed wood, metal and similar surfaces. Rinse the cloth often and bag it.

03

Dry completely

Use a towel, then a fan. A surface that stays damp invites a repeat.

04

Discard porous items

Absorbent materials such as ceiling tiles and carpet may have to be thrown away if they become moldy. Bag them before you carry them through the house.

05

Hold off on paint and caulk

The EPA says not to paint or caulk moldy surfaces. Clean up the mold and dry the surface before painting.

Do not mix cleaning products, and read every label before use. Mixed chemicals can release harmful fumes, and detergent and water is the method the EPA names. After the clean, check the area again over the next few weeks.

When DIY Is the Wrong Call

Some situations should not be handled with a bucket and a brush. In each one, the problem is more than a surface stain.

  • The area is larger than about 10 square feet, or growth appears in several rooms.
  • The water was sewage or drain backup. Stay out and call a professional.
  • Growth is on or near ducts, vents or the air handler, where the system can move spores through the house.
  • Drywall, insulation or subfloor is soft, which means it was wet well past the surface.
  • You suspect growth inside a wall because of odor, but nothing is visible.
  • Someone in the household has asthma, an allergy or a condition that weakens the immune system. The CDC says some groups react more strongly, so ask a physician before you start.

Water, wiring and gas

If water has reached outlets, panels, appliances or wiring, or you smell gas, do not start cleaning. Call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line.

What Professional Removal Involves

Professional work begins with containment. Barriers seal the room, negative air machines pull air out of the work area and HEPA air filtration captures fine particles. Wet or grown materials are cut out and bagged, hard surfaces are cleaned and the cavity is dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers.

The published standard is ANSI/IICRC S520, with conditions 1 to 3, and you may ask any company whether its crews work to it. Mold testing does not replace fixing moisture, and a clean test says little if the wall is still wet. Our mold remediation page explains how we scope the work.

Local note

Broomfield note: permits at the rebuild stage

Replacing moldy drywall is the sort of repair the city's Building Division wants to hear about. Contact the division before the rebuild and ask what your job needs, since we cannot decide that for the city.

Keep your before and after photos with the permit paperwork. They give a record of what was removed and when.

After the Cleanup: What to Check Next

Give the area a week of dry air and then look again. A surface that stays clean, feels dry and has no odor is a good sign. A stain that creeps back or a smell that lingers points to moisture you have not found yet.

  1. Run a fan or dehumidifier in the room until the surface feels dry to the touch.
  2. Recheck the spot after the next shower, rain or snowmelt, depending on where it was.
  3. Look at the back side of the wall or the space above the ceiling if you can reach it safely.
  4. Call for a moisture reading if anything feels cool or damp, since a meter sees what fingers cannot.

Do not seal the area with paint or caulk until you are confident it is dry. Paint holds moisture in as well as it hides stains. If you plan to repaint, wait for a dry reading and clean surfaces first.

Insurance and Paperwork

Insurers often draw a line between a burst pipe and a slow drip, and mold coverage varies by policy. Ask your insurer what applies, request a copy of the policy if you do not have one and keep your records.
