Flood Damage Cleanup Steps in Broomfield, CO: The First 24 Hours

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

Flood damage cleanup in Broomfield, CO begins with safety, not with a mop. Floodwater is treated as contaminated until proven otherwise, so the first day is about shutting off hazards, documenting damage and getting wet materials out before they become a larger problem. This page lays out that day in order.

Before you touch anything

If water is near outlets, a panel, a furnace or a water heater, stay out and call your utility or 911 about electrical or gas risk before you call a restoration company. Do not wade through standing water in a basement while power is on. If sewage is involved, keep children and pets out of the area.

Hour by hour

The First 24 Hours in Order

First

Get people and pets out of danger

Leave rooms with standing water if you cannot confirm the power is off. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern.

Then

Stop what you can

Close the main water valve if a supply line failed. If water is entering from outside, there is little to stop, so the focus shifts to safety and photos.

Next

Photograph and note everything

Take wide shots first, then close-ups of waterlines, damaged belongings and appliances. Write down the time you first saw water.

After

Call your insurer and a restoration line

Ask your insurer what your policy covers and what it needs from you. Call us at (303) 952-8295 so extraction can start.

Finally

Begin removal with care

Lift what you safely can, set aside items to review, and leave heavy cleanup to a crew with pumps and protective equipment.

What to Photograph Before You Move Anything

Photos are the cheapest part of a claim and the easiest to skip when you are stressed. Take them before cleanup changes the scene. Shoot video as you walk each room, and say the room name aloud.

01

The waterline

Photograph the line on walls, cabinets and furniture with a tape measure or coin for scale.

02

Damaged belongings

Photograph each item you may need to claim, and keep a list with approximate age and where it was.

03

Appliances and utilities

Capture furnace, water heater, washer and electrical panel conditions without touching them.

04

The source

If a pipe, window well or sump failed, photograph the failure point and any visible cause.

Know Your Water: Three Categories

Restoration work sorts water into categories 1 to 3, a scheme from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. The category affects what can be dried in place and what must be removed. A professional decides it, but the table gives you a sense of the logic.

Water categories under ANSI/IICRC S500
Water categoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means
Category 1A broken supply line or clean tap waterMost materials can be dried if the response is prompt.
Category 2Washer or dishwasher overflow, some appliance dischargeCarpet pad and soft materials are commonly removed. Hard surfaces can be cleaned.
Category 3Sewage, rising river or stormwaterTreat as contaminated. Porous materials such as carpet and drywall are usually removed.

Clean water can degrade to a worse category if it sits. The category can change as time passes.

Basement Flooding: A Careful Sequence

  1. Confirm the power to the basement is off before you step into water.
  2. Look for the source. A sump failure, window well or foundation seep needs different follow-up than a burst pipe.
  3. Remove water with extraction units. Pumping all at once from a saturated area can stress walls and floors, so a crew pumps in stages.
  4. Pull wet carpet, pad and baseboards, and cut drywall back to dry material if it wicked water upward.
  5. Dry the structure with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and track readings until materials are dry.
  6. Sort belongings into clean, cleanable and discard piles. Keep photos of everything discarded.

Save, clean or discard

A quick sort keeps the cleanup moving. Work from the least porous items to the most porous, and photograph before you discard.

  • Usually cleanable: glass, metal, hard plastic and sealed tile that sat in clean water.
  • Often discarded: carpet pad, upholstered furniture and particleboard that sat in contaminated water.
  • Ask first: books, photos, documents and electronics, which need individual decisions.

Keep the first day simple. Anything you cannot judge can wait for a crew with moisture meters and thermal imaging. Rushing to throw out or keep items often leads to regret on both sides.

Local note

Broomfield note: floodplains, flash floods and your address

The City and County of Broomfield maintains floodplain information for the City Park Channel and South Tributary, including a floodway between Spader Way and Compton Street. The city says it applies standards stricter than FEMA minimums, and it is revising FEMA flood map panels. Use the city's online floodplain search tool to look up your address, and check the FEMA flood map too.

Flooding here is not hypothetical. The National Weather Service summary of the September 2013 Front Range flood says the most significant flash flooding in the northern Denver metro area fell from Northglenn to around Broomfield on September 15. Use that as a reason to look up your address now rather than during a storm.

A 1997 FEMA study also flagged the City Park Channel in southwest Broomfield as a principal flood concern, though that is old data and the maps are being revised. Ask your insurer about flood coverage before the next storm season, because homeowners policies generally leave flood out.

Anyone who does carry flood coverage should open the claim promptly, and the policy itself lists its notice terms.
