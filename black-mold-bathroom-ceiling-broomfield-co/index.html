Black Mold on a Bathroom Ceiling in Broomfield, CO: Risk, Causes and Next Steps

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

Black mold on a bathroom ceiling in Broomfield, CO is dark growth on drywall or paint where steam and splashing keep the surface damp. The CDC describes the mold people call black mold, Stachybotrys chartarum, as greenish-black and says it grows on damp cellulose such as drywall and paper. It needs ongoing moisture, so the real job is removing the growth and stopping the dampness behind it.

The direct question

Is It Dangerous? What the CDC Says and What It Does Not

We are a restoration company and cannot answer health questions. What we can do is pass along what the CDC states. A link between this mold and acute pulmonary hemorrhage in infants has not been proven, and no test shows that this mold causes particular symptoms.

The CDC also says reactions vary, and that people with asthma, mold allergy, weakened immunity or chronic lung disease can be affected more. If anyone in your home falls into those groups, or if you simply have a health concern, ask a physician. The CDC adds that mold found in buildings should be removed.

You do not need to know the species

Naming the species is optional. According to the CDC, removal works the same way for every mold, so no identification is required first. A dark spot on the ceiling gets one plan either way: fix moisture, remove growth and dry the surface.

Why the Bathroom Ceiling Grows It

Spores exist both inside and outside the house, and dampness is what lets them grow. A bathroom supplies that dampness every day, and the ceiling is the coolest, least-wiped surface in the room. Steam rises, meets that surface and leaves a film of water.

Weak ventilation

A fan that is undersized, clogged or ducted into the attic can leave humid air sitting overhead. Check that the fan moves air by holding tissue to the grille.

Short fan run time

Humidity lingers well after the shower ends. Leaving the fan on after you finish gives the ceiling time to dry.

Hidden leaks above

A drip from a tub drain, a supply fitting or a roof can wet the ceiling from the back. If the stain keeps returning after cleaning, look above.

Cold surfaces

Ceilings under cold attic spaces run cooler in winter, which encourages water to condense there. Insulation gaps and a poor vapor path can add to it.

What to Do by Size and Condition

In Mold Cleanup in Your Home, the EPA sets the self-cleanup line near 10 square feet, a patch about 3 ft on a side. Larger growth or significant water damage should follow the EPA's remediation guidance for schools and commercial buildings. Use the table as a starting point, not a diagnosis.

A Careful Cleaning Sequence for Small Patches

  1. Turn on the fan, open a window if you can, and keep the rest of the house closed off from the room.
  2. Put on gloves and eye protection. Add a respirator if the product or your doctor suggests one.
  3. Scrub hard, painted surfaces with detergent and water, then dry them fully.
  4. Discard ceiling tiles or other porous material that has grown mold.
  5. Hold the paint and caulk until the growth is gone and the surface is dry.

That sequence follows what the EPA says about small areas, and it only works when the moisture is gone. If you scrub and the spots return within weeks, stop and look for the source. Our mold remediation service covers cases beyond the small-patch guideline.

Local note

Broomfield note: drywall repair needs a permit

Ceiling board that has to come out falls under the city's permit rules for drywall work. Check with the Building Division before the rebuild starts and ask what applies to your repair, so the schedule does not stall.

Census QuickFacts lists 62.7 percent of Broomfield housing as owner-occupied. If you own the home, a ceiling that is opened and closed up properly protects what you own, so keep photos and receipts from the removal and the rebuild.

What a Professional Visit Looks Like

People often expect a spray and a wipe. The work is closer to a short project, because growth that sits on damp board has to be separated from the rest of the house before it is disturbed.

Step 01

Find the moisture

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show whether the ceiling is wet from steam, a leak above or a cold spot. The source decides everything that follows.

Step 02

Contain the room

Containment barriers seal the bathroom and a negative air machine keeps air moving out of the work area. HEPA air filtration captures fine particles.

Step 03

Remove what cannot be cleaned

Soft or heavily grown drywall and insulation are cut out. Hard surfaces that can be cleaned are cleaned and dried.

Step 04

Dry and verify

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the cavity, and readings confirm it before anything is closed up.

Published standards exist for this work, so ask each contractor you interview which one the crew follows. A written scope that covers the area, the method and the drying plan is a good sign.

When It Is Time to Call

Call when the area is larger than a small patch, the ceiling is soft, the stain keeps returning or you suspect a leak overhead. We will find where the water is coming from with moisture meters and thermal imaging, set up containment barriers and negative air machines when the job calls for them, and remove what cannot be cleaned. Rebuilding the ceiling afterward falls under our water damage repair work.

Plumbing and fan installation are outside our work, so a plumber or electrician handles those fixes. We also respond to calls in Westminster and Lafayette. Water on wiring or fixtures means 911 or the utility comes first.
