Water Damaged Hardwood Floors in Broomfield, CO: Save or Replace?

By the Broomfield Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

Water damaged hardwood floors in Broomfield, CO can often be saved if the water source is stopped and the boards and subfloor are dried in a controlled way. Whether a floor is saved or replaced depends on how long it stayed wet, what the boards are made of and how far the damage has progressed. Moisture readings, not appearance, settle the question.

The short answer

Early, slow, measured drying saves more floors than fast heat or guesswork.

  • Cupped boards often settle once they dry.
  • Boards that lift off the subfloor, called buckling, are usually replaced.
  • Wait for dry readings before any sanding.
Progression

Cupping, Crowning and Buckling: The Three Stages

Cupping

The edges of each board rise higher than the center. It usually means moisture reached the underside, and it often improves as the wood dries.

Crowning

The center of the board rises above the edges. It can show up after a cupped floor is sanded too early, or when the top surface was wetter than the bottom.

Buckling

Boards pull up from the subfloor, sometimes in a ridge. This is the most serious stage, and replacement is common.

Gapping

Boards shrink and separate as they dry. Some gaps close as humidity changes, and others remain.

These stages are on a spectrum, not a checklist. A floor can cup in one corner while the rest is fine. The goal is to find where moisture sits and whether the boards can return to a flat plane.

Species, Construction and Why They Matter

Solid hardwood and engineered hardwood behave differently. Solid boards swell with moisture and can usually be refinished more than once. Engineered boards have a thin top layer of real wood over layers underneath, so their sand and refinish potential depends on how thick that top layer is.

Species and plank width play a role too. Wider planks move more than narrow ones, and some species are more stable than others. A flooring professional can identify what you have, and the answer shapes whether refinishing is realistic after drying.

How We Measure Before Anyone Decides

  1. Stop the source and remove standing water so the floor stops taking on more moisture.
  2. Read the boards and the subfloor with moisture meters, since the top surface can mislead.
  3. Use thermal imaging to find damp areas that the meter has not reached.
  4. Set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry the floor gradually. Drying that is too fast can crack or split boards.
  5. Track readings until the wood and subfloor match an unaffected reference area.
  6. Reassess. Only then decide between sanding, partial replacement or full replacement.

When the Water Is Under the Floor

Many hardwood problems start below the boards. A leaking dishwasher line, a washer valve, a refrigerator ice-maker line or a slab that sweats can wet the subfloor for a long time before the surface changes. By the time cupping shows up, the moisture has often been there a while.

That is why a visible symptom should lead to a moisture survey, not a guess. If the subfloor is wet, the boards may need to come up in sections so air can reach the underside. Pulling a few boards to dry the cavity is often a better outcome than replacing the whole room later.

What You Can Do While You Wait

  • Remove rugs, mats and furniture legs that trap moisture on the surface.
  • Lift standing water with towels or a wet vac, but avoid scrubbing, which pushes water into seams.
  • Do not tape plastic over the floor. It traps humidity against the boards.
  • Take photos of the cupping from a low angle in raking light, which shows the profile.

These steps hold the situation steady until readings are taken. They do not replace professional drying. If water reached outlets or baseboard heaters, phone your utility or 911 about the electrical risk before anything else.

Why Patience Before Sanding Pays Off

A floor that feels dry on top can still hold moisture below. Sanding too early removes wood from boards that might have settled on their own. It can also lock in crowning once the boards finish drying.

Wait until readings show the wood and subfloor are dry and stable. Flooring contractors usually want this too, and they may ask for a drying record. Keep it with your other paperwork.
