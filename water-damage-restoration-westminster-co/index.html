Local context

Creeks, Floodplains and What the City Says

The City of Westminster's floodplain page says its updated flood hazard study covers Little Dry Creek, Tributary B and Shaw Heights Tributary, and that Big Dry Creek is also part of the picture. The city adds that newer homes are not allowed to be built in a floodplain, though older homes near waterways may sit in one.

To check a specific address, the city points residents to FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer viewer, the Mile High Flood District's floodplain information hub, or the city floodplain administrator. The stormwater coordinator's line is 303-658-2370. A homeowner's policy generally does not cover flood damage, so the city says a separate NFIP policy may be needed and a lender may require it.

Westminster also holds a Class 5 rating in FEMA's Community Rating System, which the city says gives a 25 percent premium discount on flood insurance in particular flood hazard areas. Our flood damage restoration page covers what happens after storm or creek water enters a home.

Housing and history

What the Age of the Neighborhood Changes

Westminster's history is layered. The city dates its first permanent settler to 1870, the railroad to 1881 and its incorporation to 1911, and its historic pages name South Westminster along Irving Street and Shaw Heights among the older areas. Census figures put the median value of owner-occupied homes at about $532,400.

Older homes in places like these tend to have original supply lines, galvanized or copper plumbing and cast iron drains that fail at joints and valves. Newer subdivisions more often leak at appliance hoses, water heater connections and condensate lines. Knowing which kind of house we are in tells us where to point the moisture meter first.
